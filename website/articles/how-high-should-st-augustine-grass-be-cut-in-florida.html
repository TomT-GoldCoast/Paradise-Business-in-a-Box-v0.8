How High Should St. Augustine Grass Be Cut in Florida?

For homeowners on Florida's Treasure Coast, lawn care is shaped by heat, humidity, seasonal rain, sandy soils, warm-season grasses, and a growing season that can feel almost year-round. How High Should St. Augustine Grass Be Cut in Florida is therefore more than a cosmetic question. The right approach can improve curb appeal while reducing unnecessary stress on the turf and avoiding preventable maintenance problems.

Higher is often healthier for St. Augustine

St. Augustine has broad leaves and a growth habit that generally favors a taller cut than many homeowners expect. Cutting it too low removes valuable leaf area, exposes stems, increases heat stress, and can make the lawn look thin or brown.

Cultivar matters

There is no single perfect height for every St. Augustine lawn. Dwarf cultivars can be maintained lower than standard cultivars, while common types generally benefit from a higher setting. If you do not know the cultivar, a conservative higher cut is usually safer than an aggressive low cut.

The one-third rule still matters

Try not to remove more than about one-third of the blade length at one mowing. If weather causes the lawn to get unusually tall, lowering it gradually over more than one visit is safer than scalping it back immediately.

Why mower settings can be misleading

A mower deck number is not necessarily the cutting height in inches. Tire pressure, blade condition, deck pitch, uneven ground, and machine design all affect actual height. Measuring from a hard surface to the blade or checking the cut afterward is more useful than relying only on a numbered lever.

Season and site conditions change the decision

Shade, drought, recent stress, heavy rain, and uneven ground may justify keeping the grass slightly taller. High spots and swales can also scalp even when the average deck setting is correct.

The goal is turf health, not the shortest possible lawn

A freshly scalped lawn may look “tight” for a day, but the long-term appearance usually suffers. Consistent height, sharp blades, and frequent enough mowing produce a denser and more attractive St. Augustine lawn.

The bottom line

The best decision for a Treasure Coast lawn is the one that fits the grass species, property conditions, season, and homeowner expectations. St. augustine mowing height florida is most useful as a starting point; consistent mowing practices, observation, and timely correction of irrigation or turf problems are what protect the lawn over the long term.
