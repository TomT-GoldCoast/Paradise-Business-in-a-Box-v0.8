How to Get Your Florida Lawn Ready for Hurricane Season

For homeowners on Florida's Treasure Coast, lawn care is shaped by heat, humidity, seasonal rain, sandy soils, warm-season grasses, and a growing season that can feel almost year-round. How to Get Your Florida Lawn Ready for Hurricane Season is therefore more than a cosmetic question. The right approach can improve curb appeal while reducing unnecessary stress on the turf and avoiding preventable maintenance problems.

Hurricane preparation starts before a storm is named

A maintained property is easier to secure when tropical weather threatens. Routine cleanup, healthy trees, clear drainage, and organized outdoor spaces reduce the amount of last-minute work required.

Keep loose debris under control

Palm fronds, dead branches, piles of clippings, loose landscape edging, empty planters, and unused materials can become hazards in high wind or block drainage. Regular removal is easier than trying to clear everything the day before a storm.

Inspect trees and large limbs early

Hazardous tree work should be handled by qualified professionals well before severe weather. Avoid last-minute aggressive pruning that creates large piles of debris with no time for collection.

Protect drainage paths

Swales, catch basins, and drainage areas should remain clear enough to move water. Do not stack yard debris where runoff needs to flow. Standing water after heavy rain can damage turf and delay mowing access.

Secure movable outdoor items

Furniture, decorations, hoses, lightweight pots, toys, and unsecured equipment should be moved or secured according to your storm plan. Lawn crews cannot safely mow around a yard full of temporary objects.

Plan for the period after the storm

Expect saturated soil, branches, roof debris, and possible utility hazards. Routine mowing may need to wait until the property is safe and the ground can support equipment. Storm cleanup and mowing are often separate services because the workload is completely different.

The bottom line

The best decision for a Treasure Coast lawn is the one that fits the grass species, property conditions, season, and homeowner expectations.
