For homeowners on Florida's Treasure Coast, lawn care is shaped by heat, humidity, seasonal rain, sandy soils, warm-season grasses, and a growing season that can feel almost year-round. Common Lawn Weeds in Port St. Lucie and the Treasure Coast is therefore more than a cosmetic question. The right approach can improve curb appeal while reducing unnecessary stress on the turf and avoiding preventable maintenance problems.

Weeds are often telling you something

A weed problem is not only a weed problem. Thin turf, excessive moisture, compacted areas, low mowing, bare soil, and irrigation issues create openings that weeds exploit. Improving the lawn’s density can be just as important as controlling the individual weed.

Dollarweed

Dollarweed is common in Florida and often associated with moist conditions. Its round leaves stand above the turf on slender stems. Persistent dollarweed may be a clue that irrigation or drainage should be evaluated rather than simply treated repeatedly.

Florida pusley

Florida pusley produces low-growing mats and small flowers and can be conspicuous in sunny turf. It takes advantage of open areas and disturbed soil, making turf density and mowing practices important parts of long-term control.

Sedges

Nutsedges and related sedges can resemble grass at first glance but grow differently and often favor moist sites. Pulling them repeatedly may not solve the underground structures that allow regrowth. Correct identification is important because grass herbicides and sedge controls are not interchangeable.

Crabgrass and other grassy weeds

Annual grassy weeds can invade thin areas, edges, and disturbed soil. Their management differs from broadleaf weeds, and timing matters. A product that is safe for one turf species may injure another.

Why identification comes first

Before applying any herbicide, identify both the weed and the desirable turfgrass. Follow the product label and local requirements. If the lawn has widespread weeds, the better long-term question is often why the turf became thin enough for them to dominate.

The bottom line

The best decision for a Treasure Coast lawn is the one that fits the grass species, property conditions, season, and homeowner expectations.
